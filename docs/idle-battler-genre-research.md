# Idle Battler Genre Research — Design Reference

*Compiled 2026-07-28 as the research companion to `docs/expedition-idle-battler-plan.md`. Nine full profiles + three adjacent benchmarks, then a synthesis mapped to TTP's constraints (earned-over-bought, no shame states, one-sentence legibility, 8-hour rendezvous, 4-pig Sounders).*

**Selection note.** Profiled the 9 strongest idle battlers plus 3 adjacent benchmarks. Excluded: Nonstop Knight (faded; lessons covered by Tap Titans 2), Realm Grinder (faction economy, no battler), Firestone/Idle Slayer/Leaf Blower Revolution/Eatventure (derivatives or non-combat), Vampire-Survivors hybrids (covered via the Habby template).

---

## 1. AFK Arena (Lilith, 2019) — the commercial king

- **Popularity:** $1.5B+ lifetime revenue, 100M+ downloads at its 7th anniversary (PocketGamer.biz); $469.8M first year. The most influential idle battler ever shipped.
- **Core loop:** heroes fight and earn while away; return, collect, level, push one more stage, get blocked, come back stronger.
- **Combat:** 5-hero formation, real-time auto-battle; only optional input is ultimate timing. Decisions = which 5 heroes, front/back slots, vs. a shown enemy comp. Retries free and instant — "the fight" is a puzzle about the pre-fight screen (Deconstructor of Fun).
- **Gear:** 4 slots/hero, rarity + faction bonuses, drops from the AFK stream. Deliberately shallow — heroes, not items, are the merchandising surface.
- **Abilities:** ~4 per hero, unlock/scale automatically; synergy designed-in, each readable in one line, acted out visibly in battle.
- **Progression:** **no prestige at all.** The **Resonating Crystal** pools your top-5 levels to every slotted hero — the genre's most important anti-dead-team invention; swapping comps costs nothing. XP gates can't be bought.
- **Away:** AFK chest fills continuously, ~12h effective cap; the campfire collect screen is the genre's iconic warm return.
- **Roster:** 7 factions, rock-paper-scissors, 5-same-faction +25% — engineered breadth pressure.
- **Retention:** Bounty Board (long timers, 1–2 visits/day), guild hunts, 48h Labyrinth roguelite, async arena, Peaks of Time puzzles.
- **What survives without P2W:** crystal pooling, bounty rhythm, faction breadth, free-retry puzzles, campfire, labyrinth. DoF's verdict: it won on merchandising *and pacing* — the mechanics extract cleanly from the monetization.

## 2. Idle Heroes (DHGames, 2016) — the fusion-collector archetype

- **Popularity:** $500M+ lifetime, 20M+ downloads (AppMagic); ten years live. Defined the template AFK Arena polished.
- **Combat:** 6-hero turn-based auto-battle, zero input; formation order + team pick is the whole decision.
- **Gear:** six stat-stick slots + RNG-rolled stone at 40. Secondary — heroes are the economy.
- **Progression:** no reset; the wall is the **star-fusion pyramid** (a 10★ consumes hundreds of 4★). Every pull stays useful forever as fusion food — the one great idea — but the pyramid IS the monetization; **without money it's a pure grind cliff.**
- **Away:** idle drops render on the campaign screen itself — collection inside the core screen, not an interstitial.
- **Warnings for TTP:** stacked hidden multipliers (aura × star × stone × faction) make fights unpredictable; monthly power-creep releases devalue owned rosters.

## 3. Clicker Heroes (Playsaurus, 2014) — the DPS-ticker ancestor

- **Popularity:** the breakout that ported Cookie Clicker math into combat; top Steam F2P fixture for years.
- **Combat:** pure DPS ticker — one monster, click damage + bench DPS, timed boss HP checks. Decisions: which hero to level (cost curves), cooldown timing, when to ascend. Absolute legibility: number up, bar down.
- **Gear:** none — proof an idle battler needs *heroes* more than *gear*.
- **Prestige:** THE canonical design — Hero Souls → Ancients (permanent multipliers) → Transcendence (second-order reset). Resets stay fun because each run rips through last run's wall — the power-fantasy replay.
- **Away:** full offline gold; an "idle build" of Ancients actively rewards not clicking — idle players as first-class citizens, early.
- **Flaw:** the obsolete-hero purchase ladder is the genre's original dead-team sin.
- **Social:** clans + daily async co-op Immortal raid; Mercenaries (send-away timers). Nearly everything survives with money removed — the cleanest skeleton in the genre.

## 4. Tap Titans 2 (Game Hive, 2016) — prestige-as-cadence + clan combat

- **Popularity:** flagship of a 180M+-download studio; still grossing a decade in; genuinely active endgame community.
- **Combat:** single avatar + hero bench vs. one titan; pet attacks, clan-ship salvos. Build identity via a real **skill tree** (tap/pet/ship/burst builds, cheap respec) — the genre's best example of owning a build inside auto-resolving combat.
- **Gear:** equipment with rarity + set bonuses that *select your build multiplier*; not armor-vs-damage granularity.
- **Prestige:** every run, minutes-to-hours cadence — the prestige is the session. Fights an 8-hour-beat design directly.
- **Retention standouts:** (1) weekly async tournaments in similar-power brackets, rewards at all ranks; (2) **Clan Raids** — the clan burns one raid boss with limited daily attacks each, damage summed and logged — **the genre's proven small-group co-op battler mechanic**; (3) clan advanced-start: belonging = tangible tailwind.
- **Warning:** raids rank member damage — at N=4 among friends that's a shame state. Sum, don't rank.

## 5. Idle Champions of the Forgotten Realms (Codename, 2017) — formation-as-puzzle

- **Popularity:** 9 years of monthly events, 140+ champions, still shipping (July 2026). Modest revenue, outsized design influence.
- **Combat:** side-scroll wave defense; **the decision layer is the formation grid** — position-dependent abilities ("buffs adjacent champions"), each adventure imposing a different grid + restrictions that force re-solving. Only ~1 champion is the DPS; everyone else multiplies them.
- **Gear:** 6 slots, buffs that champion's *specific abilities* ("makes Bruenor's shout 3× better") — gear that stays legible.
- **Abilities:** base attack + formation abilities + ultimate + **pick-1-of-2 specializations** — a light, legible build fork.
- **Prestige:** per-adventure Favor — resets scoped small, frequent, non-scary.
- **Roster:** every champion earnable through play; events recur yearly so missed content returns — **best-in-class earned roster growth and FOMO-lite.**
- **Away:** standard accrual; earned **familiars automate clicking — automation itself is a collectible reward.**
- **Retention:** a decade on PvE + collection + puzzle variety, **no PvP at all.**

## 6. Melvor Idle (Malcolm/Jagex, 2021) — the RuneScape-shaped idle

- **Popularity:** ~15.7K Steam reviews at 90/100, 500K–1M owners, published by Jagex. Premium, zero pay-for-power — **the strongest earned-only exemplar in the genre.**
- **Combat:** one character vs. one monster, tick-based, RuneScape math. Decisions are all loadout: style triangle, gear, food, prayers, potions. Fully deterministic — "can I idle this dungeon?" is the community's core puzzle.
- **Gear:** full armor/weapon ladders, craftable via non-combat skills, known drop tables, no monetized RNG. **Gear is THE progression axis: rate + access.**
- **Away:** up to 24h of full-fidelity *simulated* progress presented as an honest itemized ledger — the genre's most satisfying away-report.
- **Bold move:** offline death with item loss — works only because fully calculable and opt-in; still a shame state TTP won't take (keep the pre-check, soften the failure).
- **Gap:** no social layer at all — a system model, not a social model.

## 7. Legends of IdleOn (Lavaflame2, 2021) — roster-as-workforce

- **Popularity:** ~29K Steam reviews, routinely among Steam's most-played "MMOs" by concurrents; solo-dev.
- **Loop:** your own family of alt characters, each assigned to an activity; per-character AFK rate% published in advance; account-wide buff web means every character's grind helps every other — closest existing model to "a Sounder of pigs each doing their job."
- **Gear:** defense thresholds gate map survivability — legible "can I idle here?" checks.
- **Abilities:** talents that *only* affect AFK gains — build choices for the idle layer itself.
- **Warning (the cautionary tale):** 30+ interlocking subsystems by W4; unplayable without the wiki; 22% negative reviews cite complexity. Retention-by-subsystem-addition is the anti-"one sentence" pattern.

## 8. NGU Idle (4G, 2019) — the hardcore rebirth machine

- **Popularity:** 95% positive of ~10.8K Steam reviews; the incremental community's canonical rebirth game.
- **Loop:** allocate energy/magic pools across feature-machines; adventure mode auto-fights for gear; rebirth for exponential multipliers.
- **Gear:** zone drops with level rolls; **duplicates merge to raise item level**; stats cross systems (+% energy speed, +drop chance) so combat gear feeds the whole economy; loadout presets per goal.
- **Key lesson:** rebirth math is tuned so optimal cadence converges on ~daily — **reset cadence IS rendezvous design**, and players experience the once-a-day optimum as freedom.
- **Away:** full-rate offline everything; "works around your schedule, not the game's."

## 9. Almost a Hero (Bee Square, 2017) — personality-first

- **Popularity:** 10M+ installs, ~196K Play reviews; r/incremental_games Best Mobile 2017.
- **Cast:** **15 fixed heroes, no gacha** — everyone eventually gets everyone; heroes are bickering characters, not stat sheets. **The strongest proof a small fixed cast with personality outperforms a 200-hero gacha for warmth.**
- **Gear:** per-hero items with quirky tradeoffs ("+50% damage but takes +25%") — small, legible, personality-loaded.
- **Abilities:** upgrades that *change behavior* ("Boomer's bomb now bounces twice") rather than adding % — the best reference for visible, funny auto-battler abilities.
- **Prestige:** hero upgrades persist through resets — no investment ever feels burned.
- **Away-report as comedy beat** ("hero decided to nap") — worth stealing.

## Adjacent benchmarks

- **Cookie Clicker (2013):** the ancestor — exponential cost curves, building ladders, golden-cookie return triggers, Heavenly Chips prestige; formalized in Pecorella's GDC idle-math talks. Everything idle battlers do with combat is this math wearing armor. Also the origin of achievements-as-content and self-aware humor as tone tools.
- **Archero / Habby template:** $2B+/350M+ across the portfolio. Relevance: the per-run **1-of-3 ability draft** is the cleanest authored per-session choice for an auto-heavy game. Its energy caps + gear fusion read grindy-P2W — take the draft, skip the economy.
- **Cats & Soup (2021):** 80M+ downloads, no combat. Tone proof: an idle check-in loop carries a cozy mass-market game when the away-report is adorable, every worker is a named dress-up character, and **nothing ever fails while you're gone.** Its flaw — no social layer → collection-novelty churn — is exactly the gap a Sounder fills.

# Synthesis

## A. The genre's 12 load-bearing walls

1. Auto-resolving combat, player-authored setup; the battle is a free-retry verdict on preparation.
2. Away-accrual chest + warm return report; 8–24h caps create the appointment.
3. Exponential curves with stage walls that convert playtime into "come back stronger."
4. A permanent meta-layer so nothing is ever wasted (crystal pooling / Favor / buff webs).
5. Roster breadth pressure (factions, restrictions, multi-team modes).
6. Role ceiling: tank / DPS / support (+ one flavor role).
7. Long-timer dispatch missions — the genre's native human-interval rendezvous.
8. Free-retry puzzle fights **with the enemy comp shown before the fight.**
9. Async small-group co-op damage pooling (clan raids, Immortals).
10. Event cadence with earnable characters; windows recur, ownership is forever.
11. Build identity via one small choice surface (tree/spec/1-of-3 draft).
12. Automation and convenience as *earned* rewards.

## B. Legible vs. opaque combat

**Legible:** one power number per unit; enemy shown pre-fight; deterministic resolution (Melvor is the gold standard); abilities that do a visible thing with a one-line description; losses that name their reason; bar-vs-bar, one enemy at a time.
**Opaque:** stacked hidden multipliers; RNG gear substats; 5v5 particle soup; power scores that lie.
**The law:** *predict the outcome ±20% from the pre-fight screen; when wrong, see why in one glance.*

## C. How gear couples to idle rates (three proven patterns)

1. **Gear gates where you can idle** (access) and kills-per-hour there (rate) — "new boots → I can now idle the swamp" is the genre's most satisfying gear sentence.
2. **Abilities can multiply the idle rate itself** — making idle players first-class.
3. **Wall-pushes raise the away-earnings tier** — the active session buys a better night's sleep.
Stat ceiling: damage, toughness, one flavor stat. Full stat sheets push players to external calculators.

## D. Failure modes

Complexity creep / wiki-dependence (IdleOn) · walls whose only answer is time×money (fusion pyramid) · dead teams/investment (obsolete-hero ladders; cures: level pooling, persistent upgrades, everything-stays-useful) · power-creep treadmills · prestige dread (cure: tight scoping or no resets) · shame states (decay, item loss, "you contributed 2%") · idle-vs-active resentment (target ~60/40).

## E. Fit for TTP

**Adopt:** away-accrual + warm postcard; dispatch rhythm folded into the 8h beat; clan-raid pooling at N=4 with flat participation rewards (sum, never rank); formation-as-puzzle with shown comps and free retries; small fixed cast with personality; level pooling / no dead investment; no resets (or tightly scoped); gear-as-access with ≤3 stats and deterministic drops; earned automation; yearly-recurring earnable event content; 1-of-3 plan drafts.

**Reject:** VIP/gacha/pity/fusion pyramids; damage-ranked boards inside a crew; global PvP vs. stranger snapshots; sub-hour prestige and tap-DPS attention demands; offline loss; subsystem accretion; monthly power-creep releases; energy meters that deny rather than appoint.

**One-sentence distillation:** every durable idle battler is *"set your team up well, go live your life, come back to loot and one good decision"* — and the pieces that made billions without P2W (away-chest warmth, formation puzzles, dispatch timers, pooled crew boss fights, earned event content, level pooling) are exactly the pieces that fit a Sounder on 8-hour beats.
