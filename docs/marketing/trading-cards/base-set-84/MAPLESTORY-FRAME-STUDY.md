# MapleStory iTCG frame study

## What makes the originals feel like cards

The original MapleStory iTCG does not use one generic illustration-and-text
template. Its frame visibly encodes how each card behaves:

- Character cards stack actions in separate horizontal rows. Costs remain in a
  fixed left rail, so the player can scan a character's progression vertically.
- Monster cards keep their attack and HP in stable edge sockets instead of
  burying those values in the rules paragraph.
- Item and Tactic cards give the object or moment most of the illustration
  field, then reserve a clearly different lower band for their second use.
- Saturated class color owns the whole perimeter. It is a navigation system,
  not a small decorative tag.
- Names, costs, actions, rules, and flavor each use a different typographic
  register. The hierarchy comes from weight, scale, and placement rather than
  extra rounded containers.
- Corners, tabs, rails, and inner keylines create density and rhythm. The cards
  feel manufactured as game objects rather than assembled from app UI panels.

The official rules are the clearest anatomy source: they identify Character,
Monster, Item, and Tactic cards and show the lower level-up action as a
meaningfully separate use on every deck card.

## Translation for Rosie's Loadout

| MapleStory structural idea | Tickle the Pig translation |
| --- | --- |
| Class-colored perimeter | Brave, Steady, and Spark keylines |
| Character HP socket | Special Character Cheer medallion |
| Stacked character actions | Named Hero Ability cartridge plus favored-style plates |
| Monster attack and HP rail | Bash, Hearts, and Guard medallions at the art/rules seam |
| Bottom level-up action | Dark, style-colored Training cartridge |
| Card-type-specific anatomy | Eight fronts for Hero, three Gear slots, Critter, Enemy, Stunt, and Place |
| Dense inner frame rhythm | Angular title joints, straight rails, inset keylines, and corner wedges |

## Originality boundary

We should not reproduce MapleStory's maple-leaf symbols, exact frames, class
icons, logo typography, character art, or ornamental silhouettes. Tickle the
Pig keeps its own truffle rarity mark, equipment silhouettes, storybook
paintings, pig cast, color palette, icon language, and card terminology.
