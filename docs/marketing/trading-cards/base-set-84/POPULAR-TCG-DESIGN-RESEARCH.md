# Popular TCG visual-design research

Research date: 2026-07-27  
Scope: visual anatomy and transferable design principles for the Tickle the Pig card game  
Sources: official card databases, rules, galleries, and publisher design commentary only

## Executive conclusion

Pokémon does not make every card physically elaborate. It makes every piece of information **predictable**, then spends its visual personality on the illustration, type material, silhouettes around the illustration, and scarce premium variants.

That distinction matters for Tickle the Pig:

- The outside cut line should always be the same simple rounded rectangle.
- The information anchors should stay fixed.
- The **inside frame should respond to the subject**: leaves can overlap a Critter window, a stitched equipment strap can divide a Gear card, and a Place can use a horizon-shaped transition.
- The ordinary frame should be attractive but restrained.
- Full-art cards should feel special because they deliberately release the illustration from parts of the ordinary frame—not because every card is covered in ornament.

Uniformity should mean “I know where to look,” not “every card is the same beige template.”

## What Pokémon does especially well

### 1. It establishes an invariant reading path

The official Pokémon rules identify stable regions for card name, card type/stage, Pokémon type, HP, illustration, text, and collector information. Those anchors remain recognizable even as colors and special treatments change. See the official [Parts of a Pokémon Card](https://assets.pokemon.com/assets/cms2-sv-se/pdf/trading-card-game/rulebook/par_rulebook_en.pdf) diagram.

The practical reading path is:

1. Identity and state at the top.
2. Character illustration in the visual center.
3. Abilities and attacks in repeated horizontal rows.
4. Weakness, resistance, and retreat in a utility footer.
5. Set, rarity, collector number, and illustrator at the very bottom.

The result is fast scanning in a hand, on a table, and across different expansions. The frame never asks the player to rediscover where HP or an attack lives.

**Transfer to Tickle the Pig:** keep name, card type/rank, primary stat, play ability, Training action, and collector data in fixed homes. Make those homes visually distinct without turning each into a separate floating app panel.

### 2. It makes the artwork the emotional event

Standard Pokémon cards preserve a dedicated illustration window while the mechanics sit outside it. Illustration rares and special illustration rares can greatly expand the visual field, but the same gameplay facts remain readable. The official database categorizes cards by Illustration Rare and Special Illustration Rare, and examples such as [Shaymin](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-cards/series/sv10/185/), [Alolan Exeggutor ex](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-cards/series/sv08/242), and [Penny](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-tcg/pokemon-cards/series/sv01/252) retain their mechanical identities while giving alternate illustrations much more presence.

Pokémon also commissions illustrations as a set-level storytelling system, not merely isolated portraits. Official coverage highlights recurring locations and artists across a set, while one illustrated evolution line forms a connected picture across multiple cards. See [Art of Mega Evolution—Chaos Rising](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-news/art-of-the-pokemon-tcg-mega-evolution-chaos-rising-expansion) and the [Froakie/Frogadier connected-art reveal](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-news/hop-this-way-toward-a-full-art-frogadier-in-pokemon-tcg-mega-evolution-chaos-rising).

**Transfer to Tickle the Pig:** Rosie should not merely stand inside a decorative frame. Her pose, equipment, location, lighting, and nearby companions should tell the card’s tiny story. Across the 88-card set, illustrations can share paths, props, landmarks, weather, and sequential moments.

### 3. Type color behaves like material, not a label

Pokémon type is encoded redundantly: symbol, color family, and surface treatment reinforce one another. Type can tint the whole frame while the card still preserves strong contrast for the name, HP, attacks, and footer.

This is why a uniform outer silhouette does not feel generic. The frame is a family of related materials rather than one universal slab with a different badge pasted on it.

**Transfer to Tickle the Pig:** Brave, Steady, and Spark should alter the internal frame’s field color, linework, texture, and small motifs. They should not alter the cut shape. A Brave card might use warm coral with quick diagonal marks; Steady might use moss with stable horizontal grain; Spark might use violet with tiny starbursts. Dual-style cards can transition materials at an intentional seam.

### 4. It uses typographic roles, not decorative typography everywhere

Pokémon’s hierarchy is compact:

- the card name is the strongest wordmark;
- HP is a bold, immediate number;
- Ability and attack names are emphasized;
- costs are symbols;
- damage values align consistently at the far edge;
- rules and reminder text are quieter;
- collector information is very small.

Personality comes mainly from the art, color, icons, and frame—not from assigning a novelty font to every text level.

**Transfer to Tickle the Pig:** use one characterful display face for names, one exceptionally readable face for rules, and one numeral treatment. Ability names should be bolder than their effects. Flavor should be optional and visually subordinate. Avoid all-caps metadata competing with the card name.

### 5. It gives numbers unmistakable jobs

HP stays attached to identity at the top. Attack damage stays attached to the attack row. Energy cost stays at the beginning of the attack. Players can compare cards without reading full sentences. The official rules define attacks as text on the card and damage as the value associated with them; see the official [Pokémon TCG rulebook](https://assets.pokemon.com/assets/cms2/pdf/trading-card-game/rulebook/xy5-rulebook-en.pdf).

**Transfer to Tickle the Pig:** do not place Cheer, Bash, Guard, Training value, rank, and rarity in visually interchangeable circles. Every number needs a unique location and container:

- Cheer: top-right identity anchor.
- Rank/cost: compact top-left or title-line anchor.
- Bash and Guard: a paired combat lockup.
- Training value/style: inside the dedicated bottom Training rail.
- Rarity: collector footer, not a competing gameplay medallion.

### 6. It uses scarcity to create visual drama

Pokémon’s premium art works because it is a controlled exception. The official card database visibly distinguishes Illustration Rare and Special Illustration Rare cards; official editorial describes new artwork as a way to collect a different interpretation of an already playable card. See [Bling Your Deck with Ascended Heroes](https://www.pokemon.com/us/pokemon-news/bling-your-deck-with-pokemon-tcg-mega-evolution-ascended-heroes).

**Transfer to Tickle the Pig:** standard cards need a strong, recognizable base frame. The six original pigs can then receive a true full-art variant:

- illustration runs under the title and mechanics;
- one or two subject elements break an internal boundary;
- frame rails become thinner or partially transparent;
- the text still occupies a controlled high-contrast region;
- stats do not migrate to new positions.

If all 88 cards are full art, none of the six pigs feels special.

### 7. It keeps the print object practical

Even expressive Pokémon variants retain the conventional trading-card rectangle and critical information inside the card. Fine collector data is near the edge, while gameplay text and important stats have more breathing room.

**Transfer to Tickle the Pig:** use a single poker-card cut shape, common bleed, common corner radius, and common safe area. Decorative frame breaks should occur **inside** the cut line. Never require custom die cutting.

## What other popular games reinforce

### Magic: The Gathering — stable skeleton, world-specific showcase skin

Magic’s official anatomy places the name and mana cost on the top line, type and set symbol on a second line, rules and flavor in a shared text area, and creature power/toughness in a consistent lower-right lockup. See Wizards’ official [Anatomy of a Magic Card](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/anatomy-magic-card-2006-10-21) and [basic rulebook](https://media.wizards.com/images/magic/resources/rules/EN_MTGM14_Rulebook_LR.pdf).

Its strongest lesson is the separation between:

- a dependable gameplay skeleton; and
- a set- or character-specific showcase treatment.

Wizards explicitly describes showcase frames as a way to capture a set’s essence. In one example, the frame begins with the familiar blue mana frame, incorporates a refraction motif from the artwork, and extends the character’s costume into the card treatment. See [Collecting Core Set 2021](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/card-preview/collecting-core-set-2021-2020-06-05). Wizards also notes that extended-art treatments push beyond traditional frame limits to expose more artwork, while the ordinary rules structure remains usable. See [Booster Fun of Modern Horizons 2](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/booster-fun-modern-horizons-2-2021-05-21).

**Transfer:** build one Tickle skeleton, then allow a controlled “Rosie showcase skin” or location-specific set skin. Make the special frame emerge from Rosie’s clothing, equipment, or environment instead of adding unrelated filigree.

### Disney Lorcana — bold corners and a generous illustration field

Lorcana places a large cost symbol in one corner, Strength and Willpower as a matched pair at the opposite lower corner, lore in a compact symbol row, and the name on a strong ink-color banner. Its official tutorial explicitly identifies cost, inkwell status, name/version, ink color, classifications, abilities, Strength, Willpower, and Lore as distinct card components. See the official [How to Play: Parts of a Card](https://www.disneylorcana.com/en-US/how-to-play-tcg) and [card gallery example](https://cards.disneylorcana.com/en-US/?cardId=2867).

Lorcana’s premium treatments preserve rules parity: Enchanted cards use alternate full art and a special rarity/foil treatment, but have the same gameplay as standard versions. See the official [Enchanted Cards Revealed](https://www.disneylorcana.com/en-US/news/enchanted-reveal). Newer Epic cards are officially described as using frameless art, rainbow foil, holofoil symbols, and tinted text. See [Stunning New Rarities](https://www.disneylorcana.com/en-US/news/2025/08/2025-8-stunning-new-rarities).

**Transfer:** use strong corner anchors and permit full-art variants to remove visual frame mass without removing information. Premium cards should never grant a gameplay advantage.

### One Piece Card Game — very large art, loud values, minimal structural furniture

The official One Piece rule manual diagrams Leader cards with power, attribute, effects, color, category, name, Life, type, card number, rarity, and block symbol. See the official [Rule Manual](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/pdf/rule_manual.pdf?20220927=). The comprehensive rules specify that color is encoded in sections of a lower-left hexagon, including multicolor combinations. See the official [Comprehensive Rules](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/pdf/rule_comprehensive.pdf).

The [official card list](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/cardlist/) shows a consistent outer rectangle and fixed cost/power/effect anchors while illustrations dominate the surface. Product documentation defines alt-art cards as special designs with different illustrations; some cover the entire card and some add foil or texture. See the official [Carrying On His Will product page](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/products/boosters/op13.php).

**Transfer:** cards can feel energetic without thick ornamental borders. A big character, a large readable combat value, a strong color signal, and one graphic action motif can carry the composition.

## Proposed Tickle the Pig design doctrine

### The fixed skeleton

All fronts use the same 2.5 × 3.5-inch rounded-rectangle cut line and the same bleed. Inside that:

1. **Top identity rail:** rank/cost, name, subtype, Cheer where applicable.
2. **Illustration field:** the largest region; subject scale and crop vary by card type.
3. **Play-effect region:** one or two clear actions, not a generic prose slab.
4. **Training rail:** visually continuous across every deck card.
5. **Collector footer:** set symbol, number, rarity, and illustrator credit.

Suggested proportional starting point:

- identity rail: 8–10%;
- illustration field: 55–64%;
- play-effect area: 18–25%;
- Training rail and collector footer: 10–14%.

These are Tickle design targets, not measurements copied from another game.

### The expressive layer

Variation belongs within the skeleton:

- **Hero:** illustration can pass behind the title and overlap the play-effect boundary.
- **Gear:** equipment silhouette becomes the central graphic; a strap, ribbon, stitch line, or clasp can form the internal divider.
- **Critter:** softer organic crop, small environmental story, nimble stat treatment.
- **Enemy:** stronger diagonal action, harsher shadow shape, bolder combat lockup.
- **Stunt:** dynamic gesture or event moment, less portrait-like composition.
- **Place:** landscape-first panorama with the title embedded like a location marker.

Style materials:

- **Brave:** warm coral, cream, charcoal; quick slashes or pennant angles.
- **Steady:** moss, pale mint, deep teal; horizontal grain or woven bands.
- **Spark:** plum, lavender, moonlit blue; star points or firefly trails.

Use one motif per card, not all motifs on every card.

### A “designed, not generic” base-frame recipe

For the next concept generation pass:

- narrow 4–6% perimeter frame;
- no thick carved outer border;
- one continuous internal silhouette rather than five floating boxes;
- title rail partly integrated with the illustration;
- subject may break one **internal** edge;
- paired Bash/Guard lockup, not interchangeable gems;
- named ability followed by one short rules sentence;
- dark, unmistakable Training rail along the bottom;
- small collector/rarity data;
- flat-to-lightly-textured materials, with ornament limited to two or three focal accents;
- ample uninterrupted illustration around Rosie’s face and pose.

### Full-art six-pig treatment

The six Special Character cards should use the same anchors but a liberated internal frame:

- art fills the full card beneath overlays;
- a translucent or opaque title plate occupies the top 8–10%;
- Cheer stays fixed in the same corner;
- the ability panel becomes a compact lower overlay, not a large parchment rectangle;
- favored-style marks live in the panel edge;
- only one frame-break gesture per pig;
- each pig receives a unique scenic narrative, while palette and lighting connect the six-card cycle.

This gives the originals prestige without making them hard to cut or hard to play.

## Originality boundaries

The goal is to borrow **information-design principles**, not trade dress.

Do not copy:

- Pokémon’s yellow border, evolution-stage tab, HP/type lockup, attack-energy circles, weakness/resistance/retreat footer, or rarity symbols;
- Magic’s exact title/type bars, mana symbols, rules-box texture, set-symbol placement, or power/toughness lozenge;
- Lorcana’s ink-cost hexagon, ink-color name banner, Strength/Willpower icons, lore pips, or ink-wash treatment;
- One Piece’s cost/power circles, lower-left color hexagon, DON!! iconography, manga-panel treatment, or typographic lockups;
- any proprietary character, logo, symbol, frame silhouette, card back, or near-identical color-and-layout combination.

Create specifically Tickle-owned signals:

- truffle/heart-derived Cheer shape;
- equipment-slot icon language;
- Brave, Steady, and Spark marks;
- a distinctive Training rail;
- pig-hoof and firefly micro-patterns;
- Tickle-specific set and rarity icons;
- a card back designed around the legitimate QR experience, with the QR kept separate from gameplay information.

## Strongest transferable principles

1. **Keep positions stable; let art and internal materials vary.**
2. **Spend detail at focal points, not around the entire perimeter.**
3. **Give every number one job, one shape, and one location.**
4. **Let card type change the illustration crop and internal divider.**
5. **Use a strong normal frame so full-art variants have something meaningful to break.**
6. **Make premium treatments visually scarce and mechanically identical.**
7. **Tell connected stories across cards, not 84 isolated portraits.**
8. **Keep every expressive element inside one uniform, print-safe cut shape.**
