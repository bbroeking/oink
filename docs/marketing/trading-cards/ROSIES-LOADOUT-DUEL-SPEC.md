# Rosie's Loadout: Duel

Status: first playable design  
Players: 2  
Target time: 15–20 minutes  
Target age: 8+  

## Product promise

Grow Rosie one card at a time, build her Head + Held + Aura loadout, rally two
Critters, and wear the other Rosie out first.

The game takes its structural cue from the 2007 MapleStory iTCG—especially its
dual-use cards and visible character growth—but uses original Tickle the Pig
terminology, rules, art, card frame, and equipment system. The historical
breakdown and sources are in
[`MAPLESTORY-ITCG-RESEARCH.md`](./MAPLESTORY-ITCG-RESEARCH.md).

## The four decisions that define the game

1. **Every card is a fork.** Play its large illustration side, or tuck it
   face-up beneath Rosie as permanent Training. A card can never do both.
2. **Rosie visibly grows.** One newly tucked card raises Rosie by one level,
   adds a style icon, and exposes a narrow action strip.
3. **The loadout matters.** Rosie may wear exactly one Head, one Held, and one
   Aura. Equipping a new card in an occupied slot discards the old one.
4. **Combat is deterministic.** There is no “battle check,” die roll, or
   arbitrary tie-break stat. Players choose targets and deal the number shown.

## What is carried forward—and what is not

| Historical structure | Rosie's Loadout translation |
|---|---|
| Character starts outside the deck | Rosie starts face-up as the Hero |
| Cards can be played or tucked to level | Every deck card has Play and Training modes |
| Tucked colors build a party | Tucked icons develop Brave, Steady, or Spark style |
| Tucked strips make a growing action chain | Rosie reads her actions, then exposed Training actions |
| Monsters attack and protect the character | Critters attack or stay ready to protect Rosie |
| Persistent items have no universal slots | Gear is limited to one Head, one Held, and one Aura |
| No automatic draw | Draw one per turn to keep the promo game moving |
| 40-card constructed deck | Fixed 18-card starter per player |
| Codes on selected cards | The same functional QR back appears on every card |

No MapleStory job names, monsters, rules wording, iconography, art, or card
frame belong in the product.

## Components

Each player gets:

- 1 Rosie Hero/reference card;
- 18 deck cards;
- 1 Cheer tracker;
- a few damage markers; and
- three reminder markers for Head, Held, and Aura.

The matched 18-card starter contains:

- 9 Gear cards: the existing 3 Head, 3 Held, and 3 Aura illustrations;
- 3 Critter cards; and
- 6 Stunt cards.

After the matched starter proves the loop, constructed decks use 24 cards with
at most two copies of a card. The smaller pool keeps matches near 15–20 minutes,
makes the play-or-Train fork recur often, and lets a player recognize most of
their deck after a few games.

The starter therefore needs two nine-up sheets per player. A third shared
nine-up sheet can hold both Rosie Heroes, Cheer trackers, slot reminders, and
quick-reference cards.

## Card language

### Styles

Every non-Hero card contributes one Training style:

- **Brave** — pressure, strong Held gear, and decisive attacks;
- **Steady** — Head gear, protection, and finding the right tool; or
- **Spark** — Aura gear, card flow, swaps, and surprising Stunts.

Styles are deck-building and unlock icons, not coat-pattern power tiers. Classic
Rosie, Orchard Spot Rosie, Sandy Patch Rosie, Halo Spot Rosie, Midnight Points
Rosie, and Saddle Stripe Rosie are all Rosie. Their patterns support
illustration and collecting; no real-world pig pattern is inherently stronger.

### Card modes

The large upper area is **Play mode**. It shows:

- name and illustration;
- rank and required Training icon;
- Gear, Critter, or Stunt type;
- slot or combat values where relevant;
- rules text; and
- flavor text.

The bottom 16–18 percent is the **Training strip**. When tucked beneath Rosie,
only this strip remains visible. It shows:

- `+1 LEVEL`;
- one Brave, Steady, or Spark icon; and
- one short reusable action or one `⚡` immediate level-up effect.

This strip must remain visually quiet. The illustration is still the first read
when the card is played normally.

## Setup

1. Each player puts their Rosie Hero face-up at level 0 and sets Cheer to 12.
2. Shuffle the 18-card decks.
3. Randomly choose the first player.
4. The first player draws 5 cards. The second player draws 6.
5. Leave room below Rosie for up to 6 visible Training strips.
6. Leave room beside Rosie for one Head, one Held, one Aura, and two Critters.

## Turn

### 1. Ready

Turn all of your exhausted cards upright.

### 2. Draw

Draw one card. If you must draw but your deck is empty, Rosie is tuckered out
and you lose.

### 3. Grow

You may tuck one card from your hand face-up beneath Rosie.

- Rosie gains one level, to a maximum of level 6.
- Restore 1 Cheer.
- The card's style icon remains visible for the rest of the game.
- Resolve its `⚡` text now, if it has any.
- Ignore the tucked card's Play-mode text for the rest of the game.

### 4. Act

Read Rosie's eligible Hero actions from top to bottom. Then read the exposed
Training actions from oldest to newest. Each action may be used once, skipped,
or left unused. An action can only play a card whose rank is no higher than
Rosie's level and whose style is represented beneath Rosie.

The starter Rosie has these Hero actions:

1. **Sniff Around — level 0:** Draw a card, then discard a card.
2. **Suit Up — level 1:** Play one Gear card.
3. **Call a Critter — level 2:** Play one Critter card.
4. **Make a Move — level 3:** Play one Stunt card.

Playing a card means resolving its text and putting it in the appropriate zone:

- Gear stays in its Head, Held, or Aura slot.
- A Critter stays in one of two Critter spaces.
- A Place stays in the single Place slot and replaces the previous Place.
- A Stunt resolves once and goes to the discard pile.

### 5. Battle

Choose your attackers one at a time in any order.

- **Rosie attacks:** exhaust her Held card and deal its Bash to an opposing
  Rosie or Critter. Rosie cannot make this attack without Held gear.
- **A Critter attacks:** exhaust it and deal its Bash to an opposing Rosie or
  Critter.
- The target does not hit back.

When Rosie would take damage:

1. each of her ready Critters prevents 1 damage;
2. her player may exhaust her Head to prevent its Guard once that turn; and
3. any remaining damage removes Cheer.

This makes every Critter a choice: attack now, or remain ready to protect Rosie.

Damage dealt to a Critter stays until the end of the active player's turn. A
Critter is discarded if its accumulated damage meets or exceeds its Hearts.
Clear damage from surviving Critters at end of turn.

Aura cards do not use a generic numeric stat. Each Aura has one readable
passive ability that changes how the loadout plays.

### 6. End

Resolve end-of-turn text, clear surviving Critter damage, and pass the turn.

## Winning

Reduce the opposing Rosie's Cheer to 0. She is tuckered out, not injured.

The empty-deck rule is the only alternate loss condition. There are no random
checks, sudden-death comparisons, or hidden tie-break values.

## The QR and digital item

Every standard card uses the same back and the same Release Party Crown reward
route:

`https://ticklethepig.com/redeem/PIG-GXF8-ST7N`

This preserves hidden information and keeps QR modules off the illustration
fronts. The route should:

1. open Tickle the Pig when the installed environment supports the app link;
2. otherwise show the App Store download path;
3. bring an authenticated player into the Shop claim flow; and
4. explain clearly when the reward is already owned, expired, or fully claimed.

The digital Crown remains capped at 10 owners across every grant path and its
claim window ends September 1, 2026. Printing more cards does not create more
digital supply. Once the Crown is unavailable, the back still functions as a
download path and game invitation.

Do not print unique redemption codes on individual fronts. A code that changes
from copy to copy makes replacement and print proofing harder, and a front QR
breaks the illustration-first layout.

## Layout refactor

The current cards are an Armory art set, not yet cards for this rules engine.
Their next front template should:

1. keep approximately two-thirds of the face as uninterrupted illustration;
2. replace the repeated Bash / Guard / Flair comparison box with rank,
   requirement, type-specific value, and one short ability;
3. reserve a clearly separated Training strip along the bottom edge;
4. keep Head, Held, and Aura color coding;
5. use a translucent text plate on the three Special Illustration cards;
6. move all QR and claim copy to the shared back; and
7. include collector number, set mark, and pattern as quiet metadata.

## Prototype build order

1. Convert the nine existing Gear fronts to the dual-use template.
2. Make low-fidelity Critter and Stunt cards using placeholder illustration
   blocks; do not commission all nine paintings before the loop works.
3. Print two matched 18-card decks plus the Hero/reference sheet.
4. Play 20 matches and log:
   - cards tucked versus played;
   - average final level;
   - match duration;
   - how often all three Gear slots are filled;
   - whether a ready Critter creates an interesting attack/protect decision;
   - actions routinely skipped or forgotten; and
   - cards that are automatic in one mode and never tempting in the other.
5. Revise rules and values.
6. Only then illustrate the second nine-card sheet and render print-final PDFs.

The test is not whether the prototype resembles another card game. The test is
whether a player pauses over a favorite illustrated card because equipping it
now and training with it forever are both genuinely tempting.
