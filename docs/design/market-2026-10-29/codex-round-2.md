# Codex review of round 2: sets, straight up, outlets (codex exec, gpt-6-astra, high, 2026-10-29)

- **1. Use fixed lots of three; keep whistles single.** Assuming equal drops within each rarity, an individual common is barely more abundant than an individual uncommon: 70% ÷ 8 versus 25% ÷ 3. At two digs/day, collecting three of a specified common takes about **19 days**, versus **20 days** for an uncommon, from empty. Four commons takes 25 days. Rarity-based lot sizes obscure this; poster-selected “3 or 4” fragments an already thin market. Display total price and price per find. Sets also strand leftovers: someone holding two must buy three more to finish. Let outlets accept repeated complete sets, with leftovers retained.

  Straight swaps are useful but require matching both possessions and preferences. “Give one lot; request 1–4 of one other find” is understandable, provided you explicitly allow singles on the requested side. Escrow offered finds, execute both transfers atomically, and cap active listings to discourage spam. Whales can still corner scarce stock; listing limits cannot prevent that. Avoid wealth leaderboards. Wishes must consume finds, or friends can recycle them for currency. Alt accounts multiply daily rewards; per-pig caps alone cannot solve that.

- **2. Orders are sound as a bounded currency source—not proof of organic demand.** Their payout creates a resale opportunity below the redemption value, but sellers can fill their own orders too. Buyers need unused entitlement; sellers need surplus or another preferred order. Identical daily orders concentrate scarcity, while four finds consumed daily exceeds the average pig’s entire production.

  **Prototype:** three common-only choices, **three finds for 14 tickles**, maximum **two completions per account per week**, at most one daily. That caps Orders at 28 tickles/week and six consumed finds, versus roughly 12.6 finds produced at two digs/day. These are test numbers, not demonstrated balance. Your four-for-18 example merely matches two ordinary Sheep pairs, assuming each pair receives +50%; fancy pairs beat it.

  Give everyone personal fulfillment slots, stagger choice menus across pigs, and let an accepted order persist for seven days. No global completion quota, leaderboard, or speed bonus. This removes the reward race, although cheap listings will still disappear. Track total issuance from Orders, Sheep and wishes against actual tickle spending that removes currency; item consumption alone does not remove tickles. Currency-source/removal accounting is standard in [CCP’s economic reporting](https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/monthly-economic-report-december-2025). Fund additional rewards through a defined issuance budget, never market-price-linked payouts.

- **3. Additional outlets, ranked by expected buying per build effort:**
  
  **First: permanent collection stamps.** Consume one three-find set for that find’s illustrated stamp; completing four different stamps unlocks an existing cosmetic. Visible missing stamps create specific shopping targets; no expiry or tickle issuance.

  **Second: furnishing recipes.** Three pinecones plus three berries make a table decoration. Let pigs choose recipes and deposit each complete set separately. Finishing the second ingredient creates strong purchasing motivation, though art and crafting UI cost more.

  **Third: seasonal Larder.** Consume sets for shared scenery progress and a capped personal contribution keepsake. Preserve personal progress even if the herd misses its target. Expect weaker purchasing: other contributors can carry the communal goal.

- **4. Make the buyer’s benefit explicit.** Show “Pay 11 → fulfill your accepted order for 14,” including eligibility, or preview the exact cosmetic unlocked. Never label sparse trading history a “fair price.” Sellers earn a surplus; buyers gain profit, completion or convenience. Use persistent goals, returning seasonal rewards and optional thank-you reactions so paying extra feels like helping another pig while getting something wanted.
