★ shop · two catalogs, one shop
Four structural answers, one catalog
Every board carries the same stock and the same states — the 1,240-snout pocket, today's drop, the Slop Club band with the MEMBERS ribbon and lock, hats and bows for Rosie, the Harvest Hoedown and Orchard Morning furnishings for the Barn, a snout price on every tile, and Owned · Wearing · Placed · Season pass. Only the structure changes. Fonts, fills, radii, borders, the two shadows and the tilts are read from constants/theme.ts; buttons are Nunito 800, as Button.tsx ships them.
★ board A
Two Doors
“The shop has two doors: for Rosie, for the Barn.”
Today's shop with the segmented control renamed to the two catalogs and /barn-collection folded in as the second door. Today's drop stays one mixed row above the doors (the daily rendezvous is not halved); the stage under the doors follows them — Rosie at rest on one, her room on the other — so the preview lives on the page instead of only in the sheet. The Slop Club band and the rarity grammar are unchanged.
Collect
Cost · low (≈ 1 build)
Trade-off. The two catalogs never meet on one screen — a set that spans a hat and a rug is split across doors, and the Barn door has to carry the collection-milestone progress the furnishing catalog already owns. It is the most conservative answer; it wins on legibility and loses on charm.
★ board B
One Aisle
“Scroll the shop; things are sorted by where they go.”
One continuous scroll, sectioned by where the thing lives — on her head, in her trotters, on the wall, on the floor, from the rafters. A pinned stage strip under the crown flips between Rosie and her room as the scroll crosses the seam. The daily drop is not a row but a TODAY stamp on tiles in place, and each section's right slot reads the room's occupancy (2 of 3 hung).
Collect
Cost · medium (≈ 2 builds)
Trade-off. It is long: ~50 accessories plus 115 furnishings in one aisle needs section jump-links or it becomes a scroll of doom. The stage flip is scroll-position logic (a sticky header whose content depends on the section in view) — new code, and it must honour Reduce Motion with a crossfade. The 2026-07-16 ruling still holds: the strip is a product shot, frozen at rest.
★ board C
Try It On
“Tap a spot on Rosie or her room, buy what fits there.”
Stage-first: the top 42% is Rosie in her Warm Plank Barn with everything she already wears and has placed, and every spot that can take something is a hand-drawn pin. Tapping a pin filters the drawer below it; tapping a tile hangs a 60%-opacity ghost on the stage with a dashed cut line — you see the banner on your wall before you spend. The drawer is a Sheet with spot chips (Today and Slop Club ride the same row), 3-across tiles, and the one primary button that states its cost and the balance after.
Collect · Connect (a room worth visiting)
Cost · high (≈ 4 builds)
Trade-off. Breadth: the drawer shows 3–6 tiles at a time, so browsing is by spot, never by scanning a wall of hats. It composes two renderers that have never shared a frame (PigStage anchors + HabitatScene anchors) and adds ghost placement of an unowned item — new placement code, VoiceOver for six pins, a stage that needs a rest pose. It is the most TTP of the four and the most expensive.
★ board D
Market Stalls
“Buy from a stall; fill the table to earn its gifts.”
Accessories and furnishings mixed by set: Harvest Hoedown is a stall with a cowboy hat, a pumpkin chair and a moon print on the same table, a tiny diorama of Rosie in the set's hat on the set's rug, and the furnishing catalog's existing 4-and-8 milestone track (chandelier at 4 · star rug at 8). Today's cart is the first stall; Slop Club Signature is a gold one. The whole shop is a row of tables you walk past.
Collect (sets are the collection loop)
Cost · medium-high (≈ 3 builds)
Trade-off. Nobody can shop for 'a hat': the axis is the set, so a player with a specific slot in mind scans every stall. The data model exists for furnishings (collections + deterministic milestones) but not for accessories — a collection_id on hats and a rule for which set a hat joins (Corn on the Cob → Harvest; Acorn Bow → Orchard) is product work before it is UI work. Mixed-set milestones also change the economy (a hat counts toward a rug).
★ recommendation
Build A now, with C's ghost as the preview sheet's next step.
Two Doors is the only board a player holds in one sentence and that the codebase already mostly holds: the segmented control, the daily grid, the Slop Club band and the furnishing collection with its milestones all exist — the new work is the stage that follows the door (PigStage frozen at rest, HabitatScene as a still) and routing the Barn door in-tab instead of out to /barn-collection. It keeps the daily drop as one mixed row, which is the shop's rendezvous, and it costs a build, not a month. What A lacks is C's moment — seeing the banner on your own wall before you spend — and that belongs in ItemPreviewModal (the sheet already freezes Rosie for accessories; give furnishings the same sheet with the room as the stage). D's set stalls are the better Collect story and should return as a row of set stickers inside each door once accessories carry a collection_id; B's location kickers ("on the wall · 2 of 3 hung") are worth lifting into A's Barn door as its section headers today.
