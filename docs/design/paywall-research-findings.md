# Paywall research findings (ingested 2026-07-10)

**Source:** Mobbin research video by Rachel How (@rachelhxw) + Jonathan Parra
(@jondeparra, "The Paywall Guy," 4,700+ paywalls designed). Studied **2,995
paywalls** + **728 subscription flows**. (Mobbin YouTube.)

## Key findings

1. **Users often decide to pay BEFORE they reach the paywall.** The paywall
   confirms a decision; it rarely creates one. Selling happens *in the app*.
2. **Multi-page paywalls > single-page paywalls.** A short value walkthrough
   converts better than one dense screen.
3. **An ugly paywall can beat a pretty one**, and **more friction can raise
   conversions.** Clarity and engagement > polish.
4. Breakdown topics: placement, pricing/packaging, **value framing** (outcomes,
   not vague "unlock full access"), risk reduction, timing, **copy**.

## How it applies to Slop Club (decisions)

- **Validates the under-glass battle pass.** Showing every locked premium reward
  *before* the paywall = the "decide before the wall" pattern that wins. Keep it.
- **Don't over-polish the paywall.** Rosie = charm/trust (worth it); pixel-perfect
  design is not. Ship a clear, honest, cute paywall.
- **Value framing on the paywall copy** — lead with concrete outcomes (bigger
  tickle bank, monthly stipend, exclusive cosmetics), not "Unlock full access."
- **Multi-step is the future upgrade** — the current template's testimonial
  carousel is a light version; a 2–3 step (what it is → what you get → price)
  is the research-backed next iteration if we want to lift conversion.

## Paywall edits driven by this (RevenueCat `slop_club` paywall)

- Title: "Unlock full access" → **"Join the Slop Club"** (brand + clear CTA;
  value already established by the in-app card + under-glass pass).
- Carousel cards: replace the "cat photo app / CatGPT" placeholder with
