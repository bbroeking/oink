# Card depth concept direction

Preserve the established framed-card information architecture while treating the illustration window as a small stage. Depth should amplify character and rarity without compromising the fixed name, rank/Cheer, stats, Play, ability, and collector zones. Rosie’s motion language is playful and buoyant: compress, spring forward, briefly overshoot, then settle with a gentle card tilt.

## 1. Layered storybook

**Composition:** Rosie, reeds, and leaves become dimensional cut-paper layers across a clearly separated near, middle, and far bog. Her ears and foreground foliage breach selected upper and side edges while all printed zones remain unobstructed.

**Motion translation:** A small downward anticipation compresses the layers; Rosie springs upward as foreground leaves lag behind, then the pieces settle at slightly different speeds with a soft two-degree tilt.

## 2. Living Legend

**Composition:** A rounded, hand-painted Rosie physically emerges from the illustration window. Her front trotters rest on the lower art rail while her ears, snout, and upper body cross the rim, creating a bold silhouette and unmistakable premium depth.

**Motion translation:** Rosie crouches inside the window, springs up and toward the viewer, overshoots with lifted ears and trotters, then lands softly on the rail as the card rocks and settles.

## 3. Bog diorama

**Composition:** The card becomes a shallow miniature terrarium. Meadow grass, clover, reeds, and fireflies occupy distinct depth planes while Rosie leans beyond the opening into screen space. Printed rails and sockets remain crisp and visibly structural.

**Motion translation:** Grass compresses before Rosie rises; she leans forward through the opening, fireflies and clover trail the movement, then everything settles with soft secondary sway and a restrained diagonal tilt.

The strongest first proof is the **Living Legend** treatment for one Rosie Legend in a separate browser prototype: existing Rosie mesh inside a transparent 3D viewport, deterministic native/DOM text above it, and a tuned spring bounce. This remains a concept direction—not a native integration—and common cards can retain the less expensive layered-storybook treatment.
