# Habitat — the inside-the-Barn decoratable space

Adopting a habitat system that bifurcates the current Barn screen into an **Exterior** view (Rosie + outdoor scene + clickable barn structure) and an **Interior** view (the decoratable indoor space). The Interior holds 6 typed slots that accept items from the existing `hats` catalog under new category values, plus an interior background. Slot-based placement (not free-drag), shop+earned sourcing (no crates in v1), and friends visiting see + tap items but cannot modify.

The framing came from the user-described image: *"we can click into the barn, it opens and we can hang out in there with rosie."* That implicitly locks two architectural commitments — the Exterior is the world *outside* a building, and the Interior is a discrete second view reached via the building. The whole spec follows from that mental model.

## Considered options

Each fork below was an explicit alternative we rejected.

**Placement freedom — slot-based vs free-drag vs grid-snap vs additive-overlay-only.** Picked **slot-based, typed**. Free-drag (Club Penguin / Sims) requires gesture handlers, hit testing, z-order tooling, save+restore positions, an "edit mode" — heavy implementation with ongoing tuning. Grid-snap is the compromise option but still requires drag UI. Additive overlays (no placement at all, just stack preset effects) is too thin to feel like decoration. Slot-based reuses TTP's existing anchor-math pattern (HAT_REL), eliminates the "chair on a chair" failure mode, and keeps content uniform.

**Space — Barn-layered vs separate Yard vs Both vs Interior-inside-Barn.** Picked **Interior reached by tapping the barn structure on the Exterior**. Barn-layered means habitat slots compete directly with happiness's mood overlay, the Garden, Hoofprints chips, ambient effects — the home screen becomes unreadable. A separate Yard tab adds nav surface and splits the player's attention between two homes. Both is over-scoped. The "click into the barn → hang out inside" framing the user described maps cleanly to Stardew / Animal Crossing's outside-vs-inside convention and gives habitat its own clean canvas without taking real estate away from the Exterior systems.

**Entry transition — door-swing vs modal-sheet vs pan-zoom vs instant.** Picked **door-swing animation, full-screen swap**. Modal sheet feels like opening a menu, not entering a space — undermines the cozy "we hang out in there with rosie" intent. Pan-zoom is cinematic but heavy and risks slowness on repeated entries. Instant cut is fastest but loses the ritual; the entry moment is part of the feature. ~600ms door swing reuses standard route-swap infrastructure and earns one-time art (the doors).

**Slot count — 4 vs 6 vs 8-10 vs variable-by-theme.** Picked **6 fixed slots**. 4 looks bare and minimum-viable. 8-10 requires 30+ launch items to populate convincingly — too much for v1's art budget. Variable-by-theme means every interior layout is its own design pass; massive scope creep. 6 is the sweet spot: 5 decor categories (`wall_decor`, `ceiling_decor`, `floor_decor` ×2, `floor_centerpiece`, `surface_decor`) + 1 interior background, with ~2-3 items per category at launch giving ~15 items total — feasible.

**Item source — shop+earned vs shop-only vs earned-only vs crate-primary.** Picked **shop + earned mix**, defer crate-primary. Shop-only makes habitat feel pay-to-decorate. Earned-only is cozy but kills the immediate-gratification path and requires hand-designing an earn moment for every item. Crate-primary depends on item #3 (loot crate / gambling), which has App Store policy implications and hasn't been grilled. Shop + earned mirrors how cosmetics already work today — zero new economy infrastructure.

**Friend visibility — see+tap vs see-only vs see+gift vs see+interact.** Picked **see + tap for flavor, no modify**. See-only loses the "I see what you bought" social signal. See+gift introduces persistent friend-state, expiration logic, gift inventory, and a clutter/griefing surface. See+interact requires every item to define behaviors — content burden balloons. See+tap is the cozy middle: zero griefing surface, items tell their own story, friends still feel like guests in your space.

**Day-one launch — starter pack vs empty+hints vs starter+spin vs empty-no-hints.** Picked **empty slots with hint markers**. Starter pack means 6 launch items that are immediately throwaway as players acquire upgrades — wasted art. Empty+hints frames habitat as an upgrade loop from the first second the player enters. Tapping an empty slot CTAs the player into the shop pre-filtered by category, which makes the on-ramp explicit. Empty-no-hints risks players never discovering the system. The bet: the empty-to-full arc is satisfying enough on its own without a hand-holding starter.

**Schema — 7 active_*_id profile columns vs JSON column vs separate slots table.** Picked **`user_habitat_slots` table**. Seven new `active_*_id` columns on `profiles` bloats a table that's already at ~30 columns. JSON column loses FK integrity. A dedicated table (`user_id`, `slot_name`, `item_id`, `placed_at`) is queryable, extensible (add slots later without ALTER TABLE), RLS-friendly, and trivial to JOIN for friend-visit reads.

## Consequences

- **Existing "Barn" screen becomes "the Exterior."** UI copy doesn't necessarily change (still called "the Barn"), but the mental model shifts permanently. There is no path back to a single-view Barn without removing habitat — the user has been told the building has an inside.
- **5 new content categories** (`wall_decor`, `ceiling_decor`, `floor_decor`, `floor_centerpiece`, `surface_decor`) plus extended use of `category = 'interior_background'`. The art pipeline now produces decor items as a parallel track to cosmetics; the daily Shop has to handle both rotations sensibly.
- **The Visit screen (#4) scope grows.** It now has to support tapping into a host's interior. Adds a sub-route, but reuses the same render logic the player sees for their own habitat. Cross-feature dependency to flag during happiness/visit implementation.
- **The Garden (#2 streak) lives in the Exterior**, not the Interior. Two distinct anchor systems on two distinct screens; no real-estate competition. The Garden ambient corner picked in ADR-0002 is fine as long as the barn structure doesn't claim the same anchor.
- **Empty-launch is a bet.** If players don't follow the "tap empty slot → shop" CTA, habitat ships and is invisible. Telemetry must include "did the player place their first item in N days." If less than ~40% of new players place an item within their first week, revisit the starter-pack option.
- **Inventory-vs-direct model is implicit.** Buying a habitat item adds it to the player's collection (via `user_hats`, same as cosmetics). Equipping an item to a slot doesn't consume it — the player can swap freely between owned items per slot. Same UX as the cosmetics system; no new mental model for the player.
- **No friend-modifies-host-state surface in v1.** Gifts, interactive items, friends-can-rearrange — all deliberately deferred. If habitat lands and players want more social, those features compose cleanly onto this foundation.
- **Reversal cost is meaningful.** Once the door-swing exists and players have arranged interiors and friends have seen them, removing the system invalidates a chunk of shop purchases and a social-flex surface. Audit habitat engagement (placement rate, friend-visit-to-interior-entry rate) within 4 weeks of launch and pull the plug early if numbers are weak.
