# Beyond the Hedge - Clover Verge Playtest Guide

**Purpose:** Test whether preparation creates curiosity-driven replay, not whether the tester can follow instructions.

**Prototype:** https://bbroeking.github.io/oink/idle-lab.html?variant=A

## The question

> After Rosie returns with one memorable thing, does the player form a world-based hypothesis, change one preparation choice, and send her back because they want to know what else Clover Verge contains?

## Evidence standard

The prototype can prove mechanical causality by itself. It cannot prove desire, memory, or wonder without observed people.

Use three kinds of evidence:

1. **Behavior:** what the tester changes without help.
2. **Recall:** what the tester remembers without looking.
3. **Explanation:** why the tester says they sent Rosie again.

Do not accept button clicks alone as evidence. A tester may comply without caring.

## Recommended sample

- Five fresh testers are enough to expose the largest comprehension and motivation failures.
- At least three should not have heard the Beyond the Hedge pitch.
- Test one person at a time.
- Rotate the starting interface:
  - Testers 1 and 4 start with A - Pack the Bag.
  - Testers 2 and 5 start with B - Walk the Verge.
  - Tester 3 starts with C - Welcome Home.
- Let testers use the bottom arrows after their first return.

This is directional design evidence, not statistical product validation.

## Neutral tester invitation

Send only this before scheduling:

> Would you try a tiny five-to-ten-minute game prototype while I watch? There is no right answer, and I am testing the design—not you. Please wait to open the link until we start so I do not accidentally explain it first.

Do not mention the Dry Bag, Rain-Glass, Hedge Bell, idle-game research, desired interface, or success criteria in the invitation.

## Before the session

1. Open the assigned variant in a fresh tab.
2. Press **Reset Clover Verge**.
3. Do not explain the Wicker Basket, Dry Bag, Wooden Spoon, Rain-Glass, or Hedge Bell.
4. Do not show the playtest criteria or verdict questions in advance.
5. Say only:

> Rosie can explore the countryside around her Barn while you are away. Please think aloud and use this however makes sense to you.

Do not describe it as an idle game, collection game, loadout system, or puzzle. Those labels prime optimization.

## Session script

### Stage 1 - First send-off

Observe without helping:

- What does the tester look at first?
- Do they read the place clues or scan the controls?
- Can they explain Tool versus Pack in their own words?
- Do they change the default preparation? Why?
- What do they expect Rosie might find?

Allow the default Wooden Spoon, Wicker Basket, strange Intention, and Poke Around trip if they make no changes.

### Stage 2 - Blue Button return

After Rosie returns, ask:

1. “What happened?”
2. “What part of your preparation mattered?”
3. “What would you do next?”

Do not say “Dry Bag,” “waterproof,” or “change the Pack.”

Strong evidence:

- The tester mentions the Rain-Glass, not just the Blue Button.
- They infer that the Wicker Basket allowed rain through.
- They independently select the Dry Bag or explicitly ask for waterproof protection.

If they are stuck for 45 seconds, ask only:

> Is there anything in Rosie's story that sounds actionable?

Record that as a prompted inference, not an independent one.

### Stage 3 - Creek Glass return

Ask:

1. “Why did Creek Glass come Home this time?”
2. “What changed in Clover Verge?”
3. “What do you think rang beneath the fencepost?”
4. “What would you change next, if anything?”

Strong evidence:

- The tester connects Creek Glass to the Dry Bag.
- They connect the ring to the Wooden Spoon, fencepost, or a farther trip.
- They choose Good Wander or Day Out for a fictional reason, not “eight hours must pay more.”

### Stage 4 - Hedge Bell return

Before the tester scrolls to the verdict sheet, briefly cover or move away from the return and ask:

1. “What was the last thing Rosie brought Home?”
2. “Name anything else she found.”
3. “What changed at Home?”
4. “Describe Clover Verge to someone who has never seen it.”
5. “What do you hope is through the opened route?”
6. “Would you send Rosie out again right now? Why?”

Then let the tester complete the in-page verdict sheet and copy the playtest trace.

## Pass thresholds

Do not expand to a second destination until a five-person round meets all core thresholds.

### Core thresholds

- **Place:** Four of five describe Clover Verge using at least one landmark, condition, or story—not “the first level.”
- **Causality:** Four of five explain why the Dry Bag preserved Creek Glass.
- **Independent hypothesis:** At least three of five choose the Dry Bag without a facilitator prompt.
- **Memory:** Four of five remember Hedge Bell by name or unmistakable description without looking.
- **Home consequence:** Four of five remember that the bell hangs at Home or the route opens.
- **Curiosity:** Four of five say they would send Rosie again and name a place, clue, creature, or story they hope to encounter.
- **Anti-optimization:** No more than one of five describes better quantity, rarity, or efficiency as the primary reason to resend.

### Interface threshold

Do not select a winner by vote alone. Prefer the variant that produces:

1. The most independent preparation hypotheses.
2. The clearest Tool-versus-Pack explanations.
3. The strongest recall of Rosie's story and the headline Find.
4. The shortest hesitation before a curiosity-driven resend.

If one variant wins comprehension and another wins affection, combine their winning hierarchy only after documenting that trade-off.

## Failure interpretations

| Observed failure                          | Likely cause                                | First revision                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tester hunts for percentages              | Preparation language feels like hidden math | Strengthen visible place conditions and verbs                   |
| Tester misses Rain-Glass                  | Headline Find overwhelms the Near-Discovery | Reduce supporting text and stage the clue separately            |
| Tester picks Dry Bag only after prompting | Causality is readable but not motivating    | Make loss and future possibility more concrete                  |
| Tester chooses Day Out for “better loot”  | Durations read as reward tiers              | Rename trip shapes and show different landmarks, not value      |
| Tester cannot name the Find               | Object lacks identity or ceremony           | Strengthen name, fiction, silhouette, and Home consequence      |
| Tester treats Hedge Bell as completion    | Post-Wonder world promise is weak           | Show the opened route and one unresolved relationship           |
| Tester follows every CTA mechanically     | Interface is solving the game               | Remove solution labels and ask the player to reopen preparation |

## Decision after the round

Write one verdict:

- **Validated:** core thresholds pass; select the interface hierarchy and move toward production design.
- **Promising:** curiosity is present, but one comprehension or memory threshold fails; revise only that link and retest Clover Verge.
- **Invalidated:** replay is primarily instruction-following or loot optimization; revise the fantasy/causality loop before adding content.

Record each session in docs/explorations/beyond-the-hedge-playtest-results.md, then record the final evidence and verdict in docs/beyond-the-hedge-design.md.
