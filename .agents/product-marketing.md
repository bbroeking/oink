# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v2
**Last updated:** 2026-07-29
**Status:** Founder-reviewed working foundation. Positioning and audience remain testable hypotheses; budget, operating model, player count, growth priority, and near-term Slop Club intent are confirmed.

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Tickle the Pig is the funny friendship pet for your actual group chat.

**What it does:** Players raise and dress a pig named Rosie, visit and play with friends, and form small crews called Sounders. Short daily rituals lead into cooperative seasonal adventures and friendly group competition without a public feed, ads, or pay-to-win status.

**Product category:** Cozy social mobile game; virtual pet game; casual multiplayer game.

**Product type:** Free-to-play iPhone game with social, collection, live-season, and planned subscription/IAP layers. An Android listing is drafted, but the live product is iPhone-first.

**Business model:** Bootstrapped free download with no ads and a $200 monthly marketing budget. Planned monetization is expression and convenience through a near-term Slop Club launch, premium seasonal rewards, and cosmetics; competitive status and accomplishment remain earned through play. Current pricing and package structure need reconciliation before paid conversion work because repo documents disagree and the live App Store has no visible IAP.

## Target Audience

**Primary audience:** English-speaking iPhone players, marketed to adults 18+ while remaining broadly approachable, who already have a small group of real friends, siblings, roommates, partners, or family members they like to share low-pressure rituals with. Creative can be women-forward around friendship, care, collecting, and self-expression without making the brand gender-exclusive.

**Primary use case:** Give an existing friend group a shared, funny pet and a reason to check in for a minute together.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Give a group chat a small recurring ritual that feels warmer than another message or public post.
- Care for and customize a memorable character, then let real friends see and affect that character.
- Cooperate and lightly compete in short asynchronous sessions without an endless grind.

**Use cases:**

- A notification says a friend's pig looks sad, prompting a quick visit and tickle.
- Friends form a Sounder, dig during a shared feeding, and compare weekly progress.
- A player dresses Rosie in a ridiculous outfit and shows it to friends.
- Friends exchange blessings, curses, trades, postcards, and small acts of mischief.
- A physical Golden Ticket or collectible card unlocks a cosmetic and starts a conversation.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Existing friend groups want lightweight ways to feel present in one another's lives, but group chats can go quiet, public social feeds demand performance, and many virtual-pet or relationship apps are solo or couple-centric.

**Why alternatives fall short:**

- Public social apps optimize for audiences, feeds, and content production rather than a few real friends.
- Many shared-pet apps center one person or one couple rather than a three-to-five-person friend group.
- Idle and collection games often create solitary accumulation without meaningful friend consequences.
- Broad multiplayer games ask for more time, coordination, or skill than a warm spare minute allows.

**What it costs them:** Missed small moments of connection, pressure to perform publicly, abandoned high-commitment games, and yet another group-chat link that does not become a shared habit.

**Emotional tension:** "I want a reason to think about my friends that is funny and easy, not another obligation."

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** Widgetable, Pengu/Friends, SumOne, and Cozy Couples — shared-pet or relationship rituals, usually optimized for solo/couple intimacy or utility rather than a small group's authored game world.

**Secondary:** Finch, Pocket Love, Neko Atsume, Cats & Soup, Locket, and Yope — they compete for cozy daily attention, self-expression, private connection, or collection, but do not combine Rosie's authored identity with small-group rituals and cooperative seasons.

**Indirect:** Group chats, public social feeds, casual idle games, and doing nothing — familiar habits with no install friction, but without a shared character, earned collection, or cooperative arc.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

- One named, authored central character instead of a generic or generated pet.
- Designed for small groups of real friends, not only solo play, couples, or followers.
- Affection and playful mischief through visits, tickles, blessings, curses, trades, and gifts.
- Cooperative Sounders and seasonal adventures that create shared stories.
- Cosmetics and decorated spaces that friends can actually see.
- No ads, no public attention feed, and no pay-to-win status.
- Physical-to-digital Golden Tickets and Rosie's Loadout collectible-card work.

**How we do it differently:** Competition exists to make cooperation urgent. The game calls players at human intervals, turns friendship into tiny visible acts, and lets the world respond immediately in pictures.

**Why that's better:** Players get the emotional payoff of a social ritual without the pressure of posting, the scheduling burden of synchronous multiplayer, or the extraction patterns of ad-driven mobile games.

**Why customers choose us:** Rosie is memorable, the premise is instantly funny once translated into plain language, and the product gives an existing relationship something to do together.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Is this just a tapping game?" | Tapping is the immediate toy; friends, outfits, visits, Sounders, seasonal digs, collections, and shared stories are the lasting game. |
| "My friends won't install another app." | The first invitation must show a concrete recipient benefit and a specific shared moment. Solo play remains possible, but the marketing should be honest that the best experience starts with at least one real friend. |
| "This looks like it is for children." | The art is warm and approachable, but the humor, group-chat framing, collection, and seasonal systems are made for older teens and adults. The product is not directed to children under 13. |
| "I don't have time for another live-service game." | Sessions are short, asynchronous, and intentionally do not punish the hours between. |

**Anti-persona:** Players seeking hard-core synchronous competition, public chat or follower growth, pay-to-win progression, an endless content treadmill, or a solitary optimization game. Children under 13 are not a marketing audience.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** Solo pet apps feel lonely; couple apps exclude broader friend groups; public feeds feel performative; group chats lack a shared object that changes over time.

**Pull:** A funny named pig, a group-chat-native premise, visible friend actions, collectible self-expression, cooperative seasons, and a no-ads/no-feed promise.

**Habit:** Friends already use Messages, Instagram, TikTok, Discord, or another group chat and may not see why a separate app deserves attention.

**Anxiety:** The app may look childish, "Sounder" may be opaque before it is translated, and a player may worry the game has little value unless several friends join.

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Cozy, hand-made, warm, playful, lightly mischievous.

**Style:** Direct and conversational. Lead with a plain-language human promise, then let the game's own vocabulary add flavor. Use short sentences, concrete images, and jokes that sound like a friend rather than a campaign.

**Personality:** Cozy, hand-made, warm, sincere, odd.

**Non-negotiables:**

- Show feelings rather than naming a metric.
- Earned status stays earned; money buys expression, not accomplishment.
- No shame states or manipulative urgency.
- No emoji in the product UI.
- Nothing that needs explaining twice.

## Goals

**Business goal:** Increase App Store downloads and launch Slop Club soon, while growing from a founder-network-scale game into repeatably acquired, activated, and retained friend groups without weakening the no-ads, no-feed, no-pay-to-win promise.

**Operating model:** The founder remains accountable for product decisions, external account access, publishing approvals, spend authorization, and regulated/legal judgment. Codex owns marketing strategy plus preparation and iteration of creative, community, ASO, launch, measurement, and paid-campaign work. Human actions remain necessary where a platform requires account, billing, legal, or final publish authority.

**Marketing budget:** $200 per month, bootstrapped. The plan should use organic, referral, ASO, community, and founder-led distribution as the operating base; paid spend is a tightly controlled learning budget, not a scaling engine.

**North-star candidate:** Weekly activated Sounders — Sounders with at least two distinct members completing one meaningful shared action during the week. Final event definition and baseline are still required.

**Primary conversion action:** Download the app. The quality guardrail is that the new player then completes onboarding and connects with at least one real friend.

**Activation candidate:** Within 72 hours, a new player connects with a friend and completes a successful friend visit, ritual, trade, referral redemption, or first real Sounder dig. The exact canonical event should be selected after the first active-group interviews.

**Current metrics:** The founder confirms 50 genuine players. Acquisition is the clearest visible bottleneck: only 7 signups appeared across the 14-day internal series ending 2026-07-29, while the existing base generated hundreds of daily social actions. Cohort activation, D1/D7/D30 retention, App Store page conversion, CAC, ARPU, and LTV are not yet available in a decision-ready view.
