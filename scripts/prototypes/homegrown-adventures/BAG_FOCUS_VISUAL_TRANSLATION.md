# Position 7 Bag-focus visual translation

## Question

Can Position 7 make one freely chosen Bag pocket unmistakable without covering
Rosie and the Farm with a stacked form?

## Reference read

Approved reference:
`assets/concepts/homegrown-adventures/end-to-end-flow/rosie-v3/07-free-bag-selection.png`

The reference succeeds because Rosie and the open Bag are the hero objects. The
three pockets read as physical parts of one decision, item art carries more of
the explanation than prose, and packing has one terminal action. It becomes too
dense at phone scale when every pocket card, alternative shelf, stock count, and
review control is shown simultaneously.

## Locked facts

- The Bag begins empty.
- Provision, Tool, and Carrier are freely chosen and may each stay empty.
- Every owned alternative remains directly reachable.
- Unavailable items state the real stock or material requirement.
- Provision and fresh Cloth Wrap lining are spent only when the Adventure is
  packed; Tool and Carrier come Home.
- An empty or incomplete Bag is safe and returns a useful Near-Discovery.
- The selected item remains physically visible in the open Bag and keeps the
  existing authored Rive receive performance.
- Keyboard tab semantics, 44 px touch targets, and reduced motion remain.

## Studies

### A — Pocket Rail

The open Bag stays beside Rosie. Three shallow pocket buttons attach visually to
its lower edge, and one compact tray asks only the active pocket's question.
This keeps the whole Bag legible while collapsing the old vertical ledger.

### B — Packing Bench

The three current choices form a vertical set of physical objects to the left
of the open Bag. The active item reads as moving left-to-right into the Bag.
Pocket switching moves to a compact rail below the objects.

### C — Item Shelf

The active pocket becomes one large illustrated shelf with three choice cards.
The Bag remains visible in the corner as the receiving object. This maximizes
item readability but risks turning preparation into a catalog screen.

## Evaluation

Judge at 390 × 844 and fitted desktop. The winner must preserve Rosie as a
visible character, expose exactly one active question, keep all three choices
understandable without tiny copy, and make the selected item visibly belong to
the Bag.

## Verdict

**A — Pocket Rail wins.** At the fitted desktop view, Rosie remains fully
readable beside the open Bag, the three pockets form one obvious horizontal
object, and the active question plus its three answers occupy one compact tray.
The selected Provision, Tool, and Carrier remain physically visible in the Bag.

B covers Rosie's face with the vertical choice bench and divides attention
between two columns. C makes the current choices readable but covers both Rosie
and the Bag with a catalog-like shelf. Neither preserves the caretaker moment as
well as A.
